## 6. Handschuh, "Apertus 1.5 business benchmarks" (verified 13 Oct) — https://blog.nlp-lab.ai/2026/10/01/Apertus1-5-BusinessBenchmarks.html
Siegfried Handschuh, publ. 1 Oct 2026, upd. 3 Oct. Zero-shot, one identical prompt per task, temp 0, thinking off. ~3,021 cases. 8B ran locally full precision; 70B via featherless-ai.
- Overall: Apertus 1.5 8B 57.9, 70B 61.0, Apertus 1.0 8B 55.3; Qwen 3.5 9B (4-bit) 65.3; Llama 3.1 8B 59.6; Qwen2.5 7B 61.1; GPT-3.5-turbo 63.2; GPT-4o-mini 65.8; Claude Haiku 4.5 71.8; GPT-5.6-Luna 73.5. 8B = 79% of Luna, 70B = 83%.
- Banking77 (77 intents, 616 test): 8B 56, 70B 62 (Luna 85). All 24 identity-check requests collapsed into one intent `verify_my_identity`. => long flat label lists are weak spot.
- CUAD (contract clause types, 294): 8B 37, 70B 42. Quote: "across 294 clauses the 8B model says `License Grant` 62 times, while only six clauses are actual license grants" (= over-use of most general label). Also "invents types that are not on the list, such as «Severability» or «Limited Liability»".
- TweetEval irony: 8B answered "sarcasm" (not allowed; labels `irony`/`non_irony`) in 9/150; 70B answered correctly in all nine.
- Abstention: on receipts with deleted date/total "Apertus 1.5 8B never does" answer null ("did not answer `null` once"); 70B null 34%, GPT-3.5 39%, Qwen3.5 9B 40%. RAFT hate speech: 8B labelled 13/21 hate tweets "not hate speech".
- Recommendations (verbatim fragments): "validate it against the allowed set before using it"; "map obvious synonyms in code, or use constrained decoding so the model can only produce allowed labels"; "With 40 or more categories it helps to classify in two steps, first the broad area"; for clear-category routing "would start with the 8B model"; for detecting absent info use 70B "or explicit checks"; "copy the text first" raised 8B receipt totals 64%→80%; thinking mode on 70B changed results only −4..+5 pts. He lists how much constrained decoding recovers on CUAD/Banking77 as open question.
- Implication [guess]: for APS (~12 chapters → ~60–100 subthemes) do chapter→subtheme two-step; never free-text generation; score allowed labels by log-prob (closed set, can't invent) + explicit abstain threshold since 8B won't abstain on its own.

### ParlaCAP (Kuzman Pungeršek & Ljubešić; arXiv 2602.16516, LREC 2026 PoliticalNLP) — LLM-teacher → encoder-student
- GPT-4o labelled 29,779 train speeches (1,000/parliament × 28 + augmentation) for ~$100 batch cost; fine-tuned XLM-R-Parla student. Test: ~870 manually coded speeches/lang, 22 labels (~40/label).
- Human–GPT-4o Krippendorff α 0.60–0.64 vs human–human α 0.59–0.68 (~400 Croatian items) → LLM ≈ human coder.
- Macro-F1 student vs teacher: EN 0.72 vs 0.74, HR 0.68 vs 0.68, SR 0.71 vs 0.72, BS 0.64 vs 0.63. Student matches teacher, beats all out-of-domain poltextlab models by +0.08.
- With abstention (conf > 0.60 → ~90% coverage; rest labelled "Mix"): EN macro-F1 0.76, HR 0.73, SR 0.74, BS 0.68 → selective prediction buys ~+0.04.
- Rare class fix: Public Lands train ex. 145→924 raised its F1 0.30→0.80.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16516v1 ; release note https://www.clarin.si/info/parliamentary-parlacap-dataset-and-cap-topic-classifier/
- Pattern for us [guess]: Apertus-70B teacher labels ~20–30k affairs → small encoder/embedding head student on CPU for all 325k. Directly analogous, published, cheap.

### LLM zero-shot on CAP
- Gunes & Florczak (arXiv 2310.08167): US bills/hearings, CAP major topics zero-shot. Acc: GPT-3.5 bills 0.63, hearings 0.59; GPT-4 bills 0.69; drop non-matching (invented) labels → GPT-3.5 0.67/0.64; **only items where GPT-3.5 & GPT-4 agree (~65% of bills) → 0.83**. => model-agreement as abstention filter: +14 pts at 65% coverage. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.08167
- Sebők et al. 2025 "Beyond Token Limits" (arXiv 2509.10199): 21 CAP, long policy docs, 5 langs (hu/en/nl/fr/it). wF1: XLM-R-large fine-tuned 0.83 overall (EN 0.98, HU 0.81), Longformer 0.79, XLM-R-base 0.76; **zero-shot GPT-4 Turbo EN 0.70, HU 0.49**; GPT-3.5 & Llama 3 ~0.64 EN. Long-only training 0.75 vs short-only 0.69. (via alphaXiv summary; check PDF) https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2509.10199v3 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10199v2
- Takeaway: zero-shot frontier LLM ≈ 0.60–0.70 on 21-class CAP; fine-tuned in-domain encoder ≈ 0.80+; LLM-labelled in-domain student ≈ teacher (ParlaCAP). Apertus-8B zero-shot is ~GPT-3.5-level or below (Handschuh) → expect ≈0.50–0.65 top-1 on APS chapters zero-shot [guess].

## 4. Method patterns (evidence → design choice)
- **Embedding + linear/kNN head on frozen embeddings** = the MTEB "Classification" protocol (logistic regression on frozen vectors); MMTEB Classification scores 57–71 across CPU-sized models (Table above) → cheap, CPU, retrains in seconds, gives calibrated-ish probabilities. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25180 (Table 2). Training labels: existing APS links / silver labels / LLM-teacher labels (ParlaCAP recipe: teacher ≈ human, student ≈ teacher, +0.08 macro-F1 over out-of-domain supervised models). https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16516v1
- **Zero-shot via label descriptions**: embed each (sub)theme as a rich multilingual description (+ 3–5 exemplar affair titles) and score cosine; use as cold-start + for ad-hoc topics. Expected weaker than supervised (cross-domain drop 0.80→0.60 in CAP; Osnabrügge top-1 0.41 vs top-3 0.65) → always show top-3 [guess from cited numbers].
- **Closed-set log-prob scoring with the LLM** (score each allowed label / single-token code after the prompt; pick argmax; no free text) = constrained decoding in its strictest form → eliminates invented labels ("License Grant"/"Severability"/"sarcasm" failure modes) by construction. Handschuh: "use constrained decoding so the model can only produce allowed labels". https://blog.nlp-lab.ai/2026/10/01/Apertus1-5-BusinessBenchmarks.html
  - Known biases of LLM label scoring: recency (labels near end of prompt), common-token, prior label bias; **contextual calibration** (score a content-free input "N/A", divide out) improved GPT-3 accuracy "up to 30.0% absolute" and reduced prompt variance. Zhao et al. ICML 2021 https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690 → shuffle label order / use neutral codes, apply contextual calibration [guess for applicability to Apertus].
- **Hierarchical two-step** (chapter → subtheme): Handschuh: "With 40 or more categories it helps to classify in two steps, first the broad area." APS ≈ 12 × ~5–10 → total > 40 → two-step. KV-reuse makes step 2 cheap (~40 extra tokens). Conformal variant for hierarchies: Hierarchical Conformal Classification https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13288
- **Abstention / selective prediction**: Apertus-8B never abstains on its own (0% null) → abstention must be computed: (a) max-prob threshold — ParlaCAP conf>0.60 kept ~90% items and lifted macro-F1 +0.04 (0.72→0.76 EN); (b) **agreement filter** — GPT-3.5∧GPT-4 agreement (65% of items) → acc 0.83 vs 0.63–0.69 alone (Gunes & Florczak https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.08167). Our analogue [guess]: embedding-head ∧ Apertus-scoring agreement, or de/fr/it title agreement → "high-confidence" tier.
- **Calibration**: temperature scaling on a held-out silver set (Guo et al. 2017, standard) for single-label; **conformal prediction** for multi-label: Binary Relevance + inductive CP gives label-wise sets with valid marginal coverage; empty set = abstain; caveats: rare labels, distribution shift (Chalmers thesis / COPA 2025, PMLR 266:214–229) https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/548053 ; https://odr.chalmers.se/items/99cd5e04-1aaa-4f56-a083-ed04651bcc72 ; LLM MCQA conformal (Kumar et al.) https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18404v3 . UI promise [guess]: "the true chapter is in the shown set ≥90% of the time (on silver calibration set)".
- **Multi-label thresholds**: per-label thresholds tuned on calibration split (max F1 or fixed precision), cap at k≤3 labels; CAP itself is single-major-topic, APS allows multiple → report both top-1 and set metrics [guess].
- **Cross-lingual consistency**: federal affairs have parallel de/fr/it titles → classify each language independently, report agreement rate (and Fleiss κ) as a gold-free metric of language bias; disagreement → abstain/flag [guess; design]. Apertus-embed card shows cross-lingual Acc@1 only 0.28 vs mono 0.80 → measure, don't assume. https://huggingface.co/andreasmartin/apertus-v1.1-swiss-embed-0.4b-bidir
- **Ad-hoc topics without retraining** (e.g. "Digitalisierung"/DVS use case): (1) LLM expands topic into multilingual definition + keyword list de/fr/it/rm (once per topic), (2) hybrid retrieval: BM25/Postgres FTS on expanded terms ∪ embedding kNN over all 325k (pgvector/FAISS, ms per query), (3) Apertus verifies top-N candidates with closed-set yes/no log-prob + quoted passage, (4) optional: user thumbs → few-shot head. Cost per topic on CPU: retrieval < 1 s; verification of 500 candidates × ~3 s ≈ 25 min CPU or <1 min GPU [guess].
- **SetFit** (Tunstall, Reimers et al. 2022): contrastive fine-tune of a sentence-transformer + head; "comparable results with PEFT and PET", trained "an order of magnitude faster", multilingual by swapping the body. Option for few-shot custom topics (8–16 examples) on CPU in minutes [guess]. https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11055
- **Explainability**: highest-similarity passage (sentence-level embedding vs label description) or LLM-quoted span verified by exact substring match ("Check every extracted value against the source document", Handschuh).

## 5. Evaluation without human gold
- **Silver labels from existing tags**: APS links on affairs (if exposed by OPD), Curia Vista/Parliament Library thesaurus (31 Hauptthemen / 233 labels, see rcds space), department/committee fields → map to APS chapters via crosswalk; evaluate top-1/top-3/macro-F1 on held-out silver; report per-chapter. https://huggingface.co/spaces/rcds/SwissParlTopicModelling/raw/main/topics_hierarchy.json
- **Blind LLM annotators**: Gilardi, Alizadeh, Kubli 2023 (PNAS): ChatGPT zero-shot beat MTurk crowd-workers on 4/5 tasks; its intercoder agreement exceeded crowd and trained annotators on all tasks; < $0.003/annotation (~20× cheaper). https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15056 . ParlaCAP: GPT-4o vs humans α 0.60–0.64 ≈ human–human α 0.59–0.68. → use ≥2 different-family annotators (e.g. Apertus-70B + another open model) blind to our output; report Krippendorff α annotator–annotator and system–annotator; target: system–annotator α within human band 0.6–0.7 [guess].
- **Bias audit** (deck requirement): stratify accuracy/abstention/label distribution by language (de/fr/it/rm), canton/parliament, party of submitter, affair type, year; chi-square on label distribution conditional on silver label; parallel-title consistency rate per language pair. Expect worst on Romansh (1.3k affairs) and Italian (smaller) [guess].
- **Agreement-based confidence tiers** as an ungrounded sanity check (Gunes & Florczak: agreement subset +14 pts).

## 7. Recommended architecture for a 27 h build (all numbers [guess], derived from sections 1–6)
1. **Index (CPU, one-off ~1–3 h on 16 cores)**: embed title + first ~400 tok of every affair with multilingual-e5-small (MIT, 226 NQ docs/s) or harrier-oss-v1-270m (MIT, best MMTEB 66.6) into pgvector; Apertus-embed-0.4b as the Swiss/Romansh variant if it wins on our own parallel-title check.
2. **Teacher (GPU, hackathon 70B endpoint)**: Apertus-70B closed-set log-prob scoring, chapter → subtheme, on a stratified 5–20k sample (by language/canton/party/year); allowed-list only, contextual calibration, shuffled codes. ~$5–50 if rented.
3. **Student (CPU, seconds to train)**: logistic-regression / small MLP head per level on frozen embeddings, trained on teacher ∪ APS/thesaurus silver labels (ParlaCAP recipe). It labels all 325k in minutes and the daily delta in under a second.
4. **Verifier (Apertus-8B Q4_K_M 5.1 GB, llama.cpp, on the VPS)**: only for new affairs and low-margin cases. One forward pass with KV-reused prefix, log-probs of top-3 student labels, ≈3–5 s per affair on CPU.
5. **Confidence**: temperature-scaled probabilities plus split-conformal sets (target 90% coverage on silver calibration). Abstain when the set is empty or too large, or when student and verifier disagree. Show top-3 suggestions plus the highest-similarity passage.
6. **Ad-hoc topics**: Apertus expands the topic into a de/fr/it/rm definition and keywords, then hybrid FTS ∪ kNN retrieval, then 8B yes/no verification of the top-N with a quoted passage. Results come back in seconds and the verification batch takes minutes.
7. **Eval (no humans)**: silver held-out macro-F1 and top-3; α between two blind LLM annotators (Apertus-70B + another open family) and the system; de/fr/it title-consistency rate; bias tables by language, canton, party and type.
- **Expected accuracy [guess]**: APS chapter level (~12) top-1 **0.65–0.80**, top-3 **0.85–0.95**. Subtheme top-1 **0.45–0.65**. Abstaining on the least confident ~10% buys about +0.04–0.08. Zero-shot 8B alone probably sits at 0.50–0.65 (Apertus-8B ≈ GPT-3.5 level; GPT-4 zero-shot CAP 0.69–0.70). Human ceiling is α≈0.6–0.7, or ~85% raw agreement on titles.
- **Compute budget**: a full re-run over all 325k is ~1–3 h of CPU embedding plus minutes for the student. The 70B teacher on 20k affairs takes ~1 GPU-h. A full 8B scoring pass is optional at ~2 h on 1×H100 (≈$5); on a 16-core CPU it would take ~10 days, so the CPU only handles new affairs. Steady state on the OPD VPS fits in 20 GB: 8B Q4 (~6 GB) + embedder (~0.5 GB) + Postgres/pgvector (325k × 384–1024 floats ≈ 0.5–1.3 GB).
